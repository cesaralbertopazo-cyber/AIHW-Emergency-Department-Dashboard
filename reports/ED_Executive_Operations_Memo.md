# EXECUTIVE OPERATIONS MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Chief Medical Officer
**FROM:** Senior Health Operations Analyst
**RE:** Emergency Department Performance — FY 2024–25 Triage & Flow Analysis
**CLASSIFICATION:** Internal — Executive Decision Support

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Emergency department (ED) performance for the FY 2024–25 reporting period shows a system that is **structurally sound at its extremes but critically compromised in its mid-acuity core**. Against a total volume of **9.0 million presentations**, the department-wide on-time rate of **74.0%** masks a bimodal reality: Triage 1 (Resuscitation) care is delivered at near-total compliance (99.42%), while Triage 3 (Urgent) — the single largest-volume acuity band in most EDs — is meeting its clinical benchmark in barely **55.38%** of cases.

This is not a marginal variance. It is the clearest single indicator in this dataset that patient flow is failing precisely where the majority of moderate-to-serious cases are concentrated. Compounding this, **414,000 patients (5.0% of all presentations)** left the department without completing care, a volume large enough to represent a material clinical risk exposure, not a statistical footnote.

The recommendation of this memorandum is that Triage 3 performance and the 10:00 AM–2:00 PM demand window be elevated to a standing operational priority at the executive level, with accountability assigned at the department-head tier, not left to routine shift management.

---

## 2. Operational Vulnerability Analysis

### 2.1 The Triage 3 Bottleneck

The triage-level breakdown identifies a clear, non-linear degradation pattern:

| Triage Category | On-Time % |
|---|---|
| Triage 1 — Resuscitation | 99.42% |
| Triage 5 — Non-urgent | 85.62% |
| Triage 4 — Semi-urgent | 64.95% |
| Triage 2 — Emergency | 64.65% |
| **Triage 3 — Urgent** | **55.38%** |

Resuscitation and Non-urgent cases — the two extremes of clinical acuity — are both well managed. This rules out a blanket capacity failure and points instead to a **specific workflow bottleneck in the mid-acuity bands**, most acutely in Triage 3. Clinically, this is the category with the least tolerance for delay before deterioration risk rises meaningfully, which makes this finding the highest-priority operational issue in the dataset.

Two plausible operational drivers, to be confirmed against roster and bed-state data:

1. **Resource misallocation at triage**: senior clinical staff and fast-track pathways are typically weighted toward Triage 1–2 (statutory response obligations) and Triage 5 (low-complexity, high-turnover), leaving Triage 3 undifferentiated in the general work queue.
2. **Bed-block interface effect**: Triage 3 patients are the most likely cohort to require a disposition decision (admit vs. discharge) rather than a rapid in-and-out episode, making them disproportionately exposed to access-block delays elsewhere in the hospital.

### 2.2 The 10:00 AM – 2:00 PM Demand Window

Presentation volume peaks consistently in this four-hour window. Overlaid against the triage findings above, this is the period in which:

- Staffing levels, historically built around standard shift-change cadences, are least likely to be scaled to actual demand.
- Triage 3 patients arriving in this window face compounded delay — queuing behind both the ambient bottleneck and peak-hour volume simultaneously.

This convergence — the weakest triage category intersecting with the highest-demand window — should be treated as the department's single most predictable point of failure, not a random overlap.

## 3. Clinical Risk & Patient Safety

**414,000 patients (5.0%)** left without completing their episode of care (LWBS / Did Not Wait / Left at Own Risk). This figure carries three distinct risk dimensions that warrant executive attention beyond the headline percentage:

1. **Undiagnosed clinical deterioration risk.** Every LWBS patient is an unresolved clinical question. A proportion of this cohort will represent genuine acute pathology that self-resolves the ED encounter without clinical closure — the department has no visibility into outcomes for this group unless a re-presentation or adverse event occurs.
2. **Correlation with triage delay, not access alone.** Given the Triage 3 bottleneck identified in Section 2, it is reasonable to infer that a disproportionate share of LWBS volume originates from patients who *would* have been clinically resolvable but abandoned care during an extended, uncommunicated wait — a preventable, not inevitable, loss.
3. **Medico-legal and reputational exposure.** At 414,000 incidents annually, LWBS represents a standing liability surface. Absent a structured follow-up or risk-stratification protocol at the point of departure, the department cannot demonstrate it has discharged its duty of care for this population.

Recommendation for immediate review: cross-tabulate LWBS incidence against triage category and time-of-day to confirm (or rule out) the hypothesis that LWBS is concentrated in the same Triage 3 / peak-hour intersection identified above. If confirmed, LWBS should be reframed internally not as a patient-behavior metric, but as a **downstream symptom of the triage bottleneck**, with intervention targeted accordingly.

---

## 4. Strategic Recommendations

### Short-Term (0–90 days) — Operational Levers

1. **Dynamic surge staffing for the 10:00 AM–2:00 PM window.** Restructure nursing and mid-level clinician rosters to shift the peak staffing plateau earlier, aligning coverage with actual demand rather than standard shift boundaries.
2. **Dedicated Triage 3 fast-track pathway.** Establish a short-stay, senior-clinician-led pathway specifically for Urgent-category patients, decoupled from the general Triage 2/4 queue, to prevent this cohort from defaulting to standard flow.
3. **Real-time wait-time transparency at triage.** Deploy visible, updated wait-time estimates at the point of registration. Evidence across comparable systems associates transparent wait communication with measurable reductions in LWBS.
4. **LWBS clinical callback protocol.** Implement a same-day telephone or SMS-based follow-up for all LWBS patients above a defined acuity threshold, to close the clinical-risk gap identified in Section 3.

### Long-Term (6–18 months) — Structural Redesign

1. **Redesign the Triage 3 care model**, potentially adopting a dedicated "Urgent Care Zone" with ring-fenced bed and staffing allocation, insulated from Resuscitation/Emergency demand surges.
2. **Predictive rostering using historical presentation-pattern data.** The hourly/daily presentation profile in this dataset is a reusable forecasting input; commission an analytics build to move staffing from reactive to predictive scheduling.
3. **Address the bed-block interface.** Given the hypothesis that Triage 3 delay is linked to disposition (admit vs. discharge) bottlenecks, engage inpatient bed management as a joint stakeholder — ED performance at this tier cannot be solved by ED-side interventions alone.
4. **Institute a standing LWBS and Triage 3 governance review**, reported to the executive team on a monthly cadence, with the current findings serving as the FY 2024–25 baseline against which improvement will be measured.
